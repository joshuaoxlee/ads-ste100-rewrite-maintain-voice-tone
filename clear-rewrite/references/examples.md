# Editing examples

These are original illustrations, not prescribed wording. Explanations show editorial choices; routine output should contain only the rewrite.

## Warm everyday email

**Request:** Make this clearer.

**Original:** Hey Sam, just popping in because I wanted to see if you might have had a chance to look at the draft I sent over last week? No rush at all — Friday is still totally fine.

**Rewrite:** Hey Sam, just checking whether you've had a chance to look at the draft I sent last week. No rush — Friday is still totally fine.

**Why:** Removes extra phrasing while preserving warmth, informality, and timing. Turning it into "Please submit feedback immediately" would change both voice and intent.

## Marketing with a qualified offer

**Request:** Tighten this landing-page copy and keep it playful.

**Original:** Your desk deserves a little less chaos. Our little tray is made from recycled aluminium and has room for your keys, cards and the tiny things that always seem to go missing. Save up to 20% on selected colours until Sunday. Shop the collection.

**Rewrite:** Your desk deserves a little less chaos. Our little recycled-aluminium tray holds your keys, cards and those tiny things that always seem to go missing. Save up to 20% on selected colours until Sunday. Shop the collection.

**Why:** Keeps the playful framing and the subjective "little". Retains "up to", "selected colours", and the deadline. "20% off every tray" would change the offer.

## Stronger sales copy without invented proof

**Request:** Make this more persuasive.

**Original:** We offer evening classes in small groups for people who want to practise conversational Spanish. The first class is free. Book your first class.

**Rewrite:** Want more practice speaking Spanish? Join our small-group evening classes and focus on conversation. Your first class is free. Book your first class.

**Why:** Gives the message a clearer opening and uses the supplied offer. It does not promise fluency, invent class sizes, or claim places are running out.

## Plain language without changing obligation

**Request:** Put this in plain language.

**Original:** Applicants should retain a copy of their submission and must provide proof of residence if requested; applications received after 5 pm on 12 June may be considered only if places remain.

**Rewrite:** Applicants should keep a copy of their application. They must provide proof of residence if asked. Applications received after 5 pm on 12 June may be considered only if places remain.

**Why:** Keeps the distinction between advice and a requirement, plus the conditional and uncertain treatment of late applications.

## Intentional literary voice

**Request:** Polish this without changing the voice.

**Original:** By morning, the house had swallowed every sound. Even the kettle. Especially the kettle.

**Rewrite:** By morning, the house had swallowed every sound. Even the kettle. Especially the kettle.

**Why:** The metaphor, fragments, repetition, and past perfect are doing useful work. No edit is needed.

## Unresolved meaning

**Request:** Rewrite this so it's clear who agreed to the delay: "Riley told Morgan they could postpone the launch."

**Response:** Who could postpone the launch: Riley, Morgan, or both?

**Why:** The requested rewrite depends on information the source does not establish. A question is better than quietly assigning the decision to someone.

## Functional text and an exact quote

**Request:** Tighten this reminder.

**Original:** Hi {{first_name}}, we'd like to remind you that you can [book your visit](https://example.com/book?source=email). As Jo put it, "A lovely little escape."

**Rewrite:** Hi {{first_name}}, you can [book your visit](https://example.com/book?source=email). As Jo put it, "A lovely little escape."

**Why:** Edits the surrounding sentence while preserving the template variable, link destination, quotation, and attribution.

## Explicit tone change

**Request:** Make this more formal, keeping the meaning.

**Original:** Thanks for hanging in there. We hit a snag, so your order might arrive a day late. We'll message you tomorrow.

**Rewrite:** Thank you for your patience. We encountered a problem, so your order may arrive one day late. We will contact you tomorrow.

**Why:** The requested formality changes the register. The possible delay remains uncertain, and the commitment to contact the customer tomorrow remains definite.
